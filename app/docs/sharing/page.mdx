# Sharing music and organs

The [Cecilia Library](/library/) collects music and organ setups shared by Cecilia users. Because Cecilia stores what the organist *did* rather than raw MIDI, a hymn shared by one church plays on another's organ.

## Finding music in the shared library

Open **Library** and choose the **Shared library** tab. Each piece shows its tune name, composer, number of verses and length, and where it came from. Press **Add** to put it in your own library; pieces you already have say **In your library**.

To narrow the list:

- **Search** by title, first line, tune name or composer. Every word you type must match, so *parry lent* finds Parry's tune for Lent.
- **Type:** everything, hymns, or organ music (voluntaries and other pieces).
- **Occasion:** grouped into the **Church year** (Advent, Christmas, Lent, Easter and so on) and **Services** (communion, weddings, funerals, morning and evening).
- **Sort** by title, tune name, or length.
- **Source:** for example, only the Open Hymnal Project.
- **Hide ones I have**, to see only what's new to you.

Many tunes are sung to more than one hymn. A piece lists its other titles (*Also sung as…*), and searching finds them too. On a phone, the filters are behind the **Filters** button.

## Where the music comes from

- **About 210 hymns from the [Open Hymnal Project](https://openhymnal.org)**, carefully typeset from historic hymnals by Brian J. Dumont and its contributors. Each one names the hymnal it came from and links back to the project. Cecilia only takes hymns whose tune *and* harmony are free everywhere, which means their composers died more than 70 years ago. A few well-known settings, such as Vaughan Williams's, are left out until they become free.
- **New arrangements made for Cecilia**, shared for anyone to use (CC0): hymns such as Blaenwern, Cwm Rhondda, Repton and Thaxted.
- **Voluntaries** such as Bach's *Air*, Pachelbel's *Canon*, Handel's *Largo* and Schubert's *Ave Maria*.

Only the music is shared, never the words.

## What you may share

- **Yes:** public-domain tunes and harmonisations (in most countries, where the composer and arranger died more than 70 years ago), such as EVENTIDE, CRIMOND or NICAEA.
- **Yes:** your own arrangements, compositions and recordings, under an open licence (CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA).
- **No:** modern hymns and worship songs still in copyright, even if your church has a CCLI licence. That licence covers your church, not publishing. Record them for your own use; just don't share them.

## Organ setups

Once you've taught Cecilia your organ, share it so the next church with the same model doesn't have to. Cecilia says how far it's been tested:

- **Template:** worked out from the maker's published stop list; not tried on an organ.
- **Learned:** every stop captured with *Learn every stop*.
- **Verified:** learned, and a recording played back correctly.

## Sharing from Cecilia

You can share straight from Cecilia, with no GitHub account.

1. **Sign in once.** In **Settings**, under **Cecilia Community**, type your email and press **Send code**. Type the six-digit code from the email and press **Sign in**. You can add your name and church, which is how other churches see you.
2. **Share an organ setup:** open it on the **Organs** page and press **Share with other churches**. Check the maker, model, series, version and years, and add anything another church should do first, such as *set MIDI to mode B*.
3. **Share music:** in the **Library**, open a piece's menu and choose **Share with other churches**. Choose its licence and tick *I'm sure this music is free to share*.

**Everything is checked by a moderator before it's published.** Under **What you've shared** in **Settings**, each item shows whether it's *waiting for review*, *published*, or *not accepted*, with the reason so you can put it right.

### Organists' names

A shared recording names the organist only if they've agreed: the switch *OK to name them when a recording is shared* in **Settings → Organists**. Otherwise it says *A Cecilia organist*. Ask each organist before turning it on.

> The Cecilia Community service is new. While it's being tested it runs on the developer's own computer, so sharing from Cecilia isn't open to everyone yet. Until then, contributions can go to the [cecilia-library](https://github.com/garethcheyne/cecilia-library) project on GitHub, whose contributing guide explains each step.
